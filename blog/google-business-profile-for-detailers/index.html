Google Business Profile tips for auto detailers

Your Google Business Profile is often the first thing people see, before they reach your website. The notes below follow Google's own rules for how a business should represent itself, plus the practical parts a customer needs. Nothing here promises a ranking, a number of calls, or a map-pack position. Those move, and they are not something a checklist can honestly guarantee.

Google publishes the rules here: Guidelines for representing your business on Google (opens in a new tab). If a tip on this page and that document ever disagree, follow Google.

Use the real business name

The name on the profile should be the name you use in the real world. Do not add cities or services to it. "SEI Auto Detailing" is a name. "SEI Auto Detailing Best Ceramic Warwick Cheap" is a keyword string, and Google's guidelines tell businesses not to do that. The same name should appear on your website, your invoices, and the profile.

Match the profile to how customers reach you

If customers come to a shop, use that address and the hours you keep. If you go to them and you do not have a storefront, set the profile up as a service-area business and list the areas you serve. Do not use a virtual office, a post office box, or a borrowed address as if people can visit you there. Google's guidelines are clear on that, and a customer who drives to an address that is not a shop will not book you twice.

The areas on the profile and the areas on your website should be the same list. If the site says mobile-only and the profile shows a shop, fix one of them.

Categories and services

Choose the most specific primary category that fits. Auto detailing is a real category. Add a secondary category only if you truly offer that work. Then add your services with short descriptions in normal language. A coating and a maintenance wash should not share one vague line.

Photos of your work

Upload photos you took: finished cars, the shop or the van if you have one, and you or your team if you are willing to be in the photo. Skip stock images. Skip a logo stretched into a cover photo if you have real work to show. Update the set when you have new jobs. You do not need a daily upload.

Reviews, without games

Ask recent customers to leave a review on Google. Do not offer incentives that violate Google's review policies, and do not post reviews yourself from other accounts. Reply to the reviews you get, including the critical ones, without arguing out a private complaint in public. If you have no reviews yet, the right move is to do good work and ask. It is not to write them.

Posts only when something is true

Use posts for a real update: a schedule change around a storm, a coating option you actually offer this month, a week you are booked. Do not post the same sentence every day. An empty post feed is fine. A misleading one is not.

Point the profile at a website that agrees with it

Add your website URL. The site should repeat the same services, the same area, and the same phone number. A profile that promises same-day mobile work and a site that never mentions mobile will lose people in the gap. If you do not have a site yet, you can still keep the profile accurate, and you can ask for a site without pretending the Facebook page is a finished website.
